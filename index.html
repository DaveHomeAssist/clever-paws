Core principles

Reward

Cats train through reward, not correction. Punishment damages trust and rarely changes behavior.

Keep it short

Sessions should be 2 to 5 minutes, ending while the cat is still interested.

Mark clearly

Use a clicker or a short word like “yes” the instant the behavior happens.

Pay well

Plain kibble is rarely enough. Try freeze dried chicken, tuna flakes, or lickable treats.

Time it right

Train before meals, not after. A full cat is an uninterested cat.

Name Recognition

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat looks at you when you say their name.

Difficulty 1/33 to 7 days
  1. Say the name once in a calm tone.
  2. The moment the cat looks toward you, mark and reward.
  3. Repeat 5 times per session, twice a day.
  4. Gradually increase distance and distraction.
Common mistakes

Repeating the name multiple times before rewarding. Using the name for negative things like vet or bath makes it stop being a positive cue.

Sit

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat sits on verbal or hand cue.

Difficulty 1/31 to 2 weeks
  1. Hold a treat just above and slightly behind the cat's head.
  2. As the head tilts up, the rear naturally lowers.
  3. Mark the moment the rear touches the floor, then reward.
  4. Add the cue word once the motion is reliable.
Common mistakes

Pushing the cat's rear down. Holding the treat too high makes the cat jump instead of sit.

Come When Called

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat comes to you from across the room or another room.

Difficulty 2/32 to 4 weeks
  1. Start one meter away. Say the cue, mark and reward arrival.
  2. Increase distance gradually, same room first.
  3. Practice from another room only after in-room recall is reliable.
  4. Always reward, even months later. Recall fades without maintenance.
Common mistakes

Calling the cat for things they dislike. Calling and then giving up if they do not come teaches that the cue is optional.

Carrier Comfort

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat enters the carrier voluntarily and stays calm inside.

Difficulty 2/32 to 6 weeks
  1. Leave the carrier out full-time with the door open and a soft bed inside.
  2. Feed meals near it, then inside it.
  3. Reward any voluntary entry. Never close the door during early sessions.
  4. Add short closed-door durations only after the cat enters willingly.
Common mistakes

Only producing the carrier before vet visits. Forcing the cat in can undo weeks of progress in seconds.

Harness and Leash

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat tolerates harness and walks on a loose leash indoors or in a quiet outdoor space.

Difficulty 3/31 to 3 months
  1. Let the cat sniff the harness. Reward interest.
  2. Drape it over the back without fastening. Reward calm.
  3. Fasten loosely for 10 seconds. Build duration over days.
  4. Add the leash indoors before any outdoor attempt.
Common mistakes

Going outside too early. Pulling on the leash causes cats to freeze or panic. They do not heel like dogs.

Stop Counter Surfing

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat chooses not to jump on kitchen counters.

Difficulty 3/3Ongoing management
  1. Remove the reward: no food left on counters.
  2. Provide a tall, legal perch nearby with a better view.
  3. Reward the cat heavily for using the legal perch.
  4. Interrupt counter jumps neutrally by placing the cat on the perch, with no scolding.
Common mistakes

Spray bottles and loud noises damage trust. The cat just learns to wait until you leave the room.

Target Training

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat touches nose to a target (fist or stick) on cue.

Difficulty 2/31 to 3 weeks
  1. Offer a closed fist a few inches from the cat's nose. Curiosity does the rest.
  2. The instant the nose touches the fist, mark and reward.
  3. Build duration by waiting half a second longer before marking.
  4. Add the cue word "target" once the touch is reliable. Generalize to a stick for distance work.
Common mistakes

Pushing the fist into the cat. Marking late, after the cat pulls away. Skipping the cue and adding it before the behavior is consistent.

Reduce Play Biting

◯ Not started

Goal: Cat plays without biting hands or feet.

Difficulty 2/32 to 6 weeks
  1. Never use hands as toys, even with kittens.
  2. Use wand toys to keep hands out of the prey path.
  3. End the play session calmly the moment teeth touch skin. Walk away for 30 seconds.
  4. Schedule two real play sessions per day. Most biting is unmet predatory need.
Common mistakes

Wiggling fingers to wind up the cat. Punishing after the bite is too late, and the cat learns hands are unpredictable.

When to call a professional

  • Aggression that draws blood, or aggression toward a specific household member.
  • Sudden behavior change in an adult cat. Rule out medical first, vet before trainer.
  • House soiling that persists after litter box and medical issues are ruled out.
  • Severe fear that does not improve over several weeks of patient work.
  • Look for a certified feline behavior consultant through IAABC or a veterinary behaviorist through DACVB.
Clever Paws is a reference, not veterinary advice. Behavior changes can have medical causes. When in doubt, see your vet first.
